## 5. In-Depth Analysis: Psychological Effects of 1-10 Random Numbers

### Cognitive Psychology Perspective
Harvard University's decision science professor Ellen Langer's "illusion of control" theory perfectly explains why the **1-10 random number generator** is popular. People always feel they can "influence" random results (though actually they can't). This illusion gives people a greater sense of control, thereby reducing decision anxiety.

In my user interviews, many people mentioned that after using random numbers, the team atmosphere became more harmonious. Why? **Q2: Are random number generators really "random"?**
A: Good question! Random numbers generated by computers are actually "pseudo-random numbers," but for daily use, they're completely sufficient. I recommend tools based on cryptographically secure random number generators (CSPRNG), which have higher quality random numbers and are harder to predict.

**Q3: What should I do if the random number result is clearly unreasonable?**
A: This is why I call **1-10 random numbers** "auxiliary tools" rather than "ultimate answers." If the result is obviously absurd (e.g., randomly selecting lunch but choosing food you don't eat), then your candidate list itself has problems. Re-examine the options, delete obviously inappropriate ones, and randomize again.
