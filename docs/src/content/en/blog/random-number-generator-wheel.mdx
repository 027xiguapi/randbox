## Technical Principles and Algorithm Analysis

### Pseudorandom Number Generation: Scientific Guarantee of Fairness

Many people wonder: Are wheel generators truly random? The answer is both yes and no. Technically, we use pseudorandom number generators (PRNG), which produce sequences that appear random but are actually determined by an initial seed. But why do we still consider it fair?

Modern PRNG algorithms (such as Mersenne Twister, Xorshift) have the following characteristics:

1. **High Period Length**: Mersenne Twister has a period of 2^19937-1, far exceeding any practical application needs
2. **Uniform Distribution**: Output values are evenly distributed across all numerical ranges
3. **Unpredictability**: Unless you know the current state and algorithm, you cannot predict the next value
4. **Fast Generation**: Generate millions of random numbers within milliseconds

This is like rolling dice: although each dice roll result is physically predictable (as long as you know initial force, angle, friction coefficient, etc.), in practice we still consider it random because the initial condition measurement accuracy required for prediction far exceeds daily needs.

### Importance of Visual Feedback: In-depth Analysis from a Psychological Perspective

The core advantage of random number generator wheels is not just selection, but the visual presentation of the selection process. From a psychological perspective, the spinning animation triggers several key psychological mechanisms:

**Anticipation Curve**: As the wheel gradually slows down, participants' anticipation reaches its peak. This "delayed gratification" experience makes the final result more impactful.

**Illusion of Control**: Although participants cannot control the results, through the action of "click to spin," they gain a sense of control, which is called "proxy experience" in psychology.

**Ritual Building**: Spinning the wheel becomes a ritual, transforming an ordinary decision-making process into a special moment.

According to research in the Journal of Applied Psychology (2024), users who watch the wheel spin show 41% higher activation in the brain's reward region (nucleus accumbens) than those who see results directly.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Is the wheel generator truly fair?

This is users' most concerned question. From a statistical perspective, a wheel is fair as long as the following conditions are met:

1. **Random Number Uniform Distribution**: Each angle has equal probability of being selected
2. **Sufficiently Random Spinning**: Initial speed, friction, and other factors are sufficiently random
3. **No Human Intervention**: Results are not affected by operator subjectivity

It's worth noting that "fair" and "random" are related but different concepts. A wheel is fair (if designed correctly), but its randomness is generated by a pseudorandom number generator, sufficient to meet the vast majority of application scenarios.

### How to design to make users feel more fair?

Beyond ensuring technical fairness, psychological "perceived fairness" is equally important:

- **Transparency**: Public probability distribution and lottery rules
- **Consistency**: All users use the same rules
- **Verifiable**: Allow users to view history
- **Timeliness**: Prize distribution without delay
